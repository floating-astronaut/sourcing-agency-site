## What Certifications Do You Need to Import Shilajit from India?

To legally import Shilajit into the United States, European Union, or other major markets, you need several certifications. The US FDA requires that Shilajit be classified as a dietary supplement under DSHEA (Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act). Your Indian supplier must provide a Certificate of Analysis (COA) from an accredited third-party lab, an FDA registration number for their manufacturing facility, and a Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) certificate.

For EU markets, you need Novel Food authorization under Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, which requires a safety dossier demonstrating the product's history of use. Key documents to request from your supplier include Heavy metals test report (lead, mercury, arsenic, cadmium), Microbial contamination certificate, Fulvic acid content analysis (minimum 60% for premium grade), FSSAI license number (Food Safety and Standards Authority of India), and Export inspection certificate from India's Export Inspection Council.

## How Do You Find Reliable Shilajit Suppliers in India?

Finding trustworthy suppliers is the biggest challenge for international buyers. The Indian Shilajit market has significant quality variation, with studies showing that up to 40% of commercially available Shilajit products contain adulterants or fillers. Here is a systematic approach to supplier verification.

Start with industry directories like APEDA (Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority) and IndiaMART, but treat these as starting points only. Request factory audits, either in person or through third-party inspection services like SGS, Bureau Veritas, or Intertek. Verify the supplier's export history through India's Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics (DGCI&S) trade data. Ask for references from existing international clients and actually contact them. Check if the supplier has experience with your target market's specific regulations.

Red flags to watch for include suppliers who cannot provide third-party lab reports, unusually low pricing (premium Shilajit resin costs $80-150 per kg at source), no physical factory address verifiable on Google Maps, and reluctance to allow factory inspections.

Understanding the true cost structure helps you negotiate better and avoid scams. Here is a breakdown of typical costs for premium-grade Shilajit resin.

Raw material cost at source is between $80 to $150 per kg depending on grade and season. Processing and purification adds $20 to $40 per kg. Quality testing and certification runs $5 to $15 per kg. ## What Is the Step-by-Step Import Process?

The import process from India typically takes 45 to 90 days from initial order to delivery. Here are the key stages.

Stage 1 is Product Specification which takes 1 to 2 weeks. Define your exact requirements including grade, form (resin, powder, capsule), fulvic acid content, packaging specifications, and order quantity.

Stage 2 is Supplier Selection and Sampling which takes 2 to 3 weeks. Request samples from 3 to 5 shortlisted suppliers. Send samples to an independent lab for testing. Compare quality, pricing, and compliance documentation.

Stage 3 is Order Confirmation and Production which takes 3 to 6 weeks. Finalize pricing and payment terms (typically 30% advance, 70% against Bill of Lading). Supplier begins production and arranges pre-shipment inspection.

Stage 4 is Export Documentation which takes 1 to 2 weeks. Your supplier or export partner handles commercial invoice, packing list, Bill of Lading, Certificate of Origin, phytosanitary certificate, and FDA Prior Notice filing.
